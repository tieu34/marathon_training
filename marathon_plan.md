# 16-Week Marathon Training Plan with Strength & Nutrition

*4 days running/week (Tue, Thu, Sat long run, Sun easy) + 2 days strength training. Goal: finish strong, hit 3:50, and lose 10–15 lbs safely.*

**Race Day: Sunday, October 18, 2026**
**Training Block Starts: Monday, June 29, 2026**

---

## Target Race Time: 3:50:00

| Pace Zone | Target Pace (min/mile) | Target Pace (min/km) | Used For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Marathon Pace (MP)** | 8:46/mi | 5:27/km | Race day; back-half segments of key long runs (weeks 11+) |
| **Tempo/Threshold Pace (TP)** | 8:00–8:15/mi | 4:58–5:08/km | Tuesday tempo/threshold workouts |
| **Easy Pace (EP)** | 9:45–10:15/mi | 6:03–6:22/km | All easy/recovery runs, warm-ups, cooldowns |
| **Speed/Interval Pace** | ~7:30–7:40/mi (800m-1mi repeats) | ~4:40–4:45/km | Shorter speed reps, used sparingly |

These are estimates based on a 3:50 marathon goal (using standard race-pace equivalency charts) and assume reasonably even pacing. **Recalibrate after a tune-up race** — aim for a 10K or half marathon around **Aug 24–31 (week 9-10)** — plug your actual result into a pace calculator to refine these numbers before your peak weeks (Sept 21 onward).

## How to Use This Plan

- **Running days:** Tuesday (speed/tempo), Thursday (easy/moderate), Saturday (long run), Sunday (recovery run or rest)
- **Strength days:** Monday and Wednesday (or Friday), 30–40 min sessions
- **Rest/cross-train:** Friday (light yoga, walk, or full rest)
- Use the pace table above for every workout below — EP, TP, and MP refer back to those numbers.
- **Listen to your body.** If you feel pain (not just fatigue), take an extra rest day. Adjust mileage down 10-20% if needed — consistency beats heroics.

## Nutrition Plan for Sustainable Fat Loss (10–15 lbs) During Marathon Training

Losing weight while training for a marathon is a balancing act: too aggressive a deficit impairs recovery, increases injury risk, and hurts performance. This plan targets a **modest, sustainable deficit (~300–500 calories/day)**, which combined with marathon training's calorie burn should produce roughly **0.5–1 lb/week loss** — landing you in the 10-15 lb range over the 16-week block, give or take.

### General Principles
- **Don't under-fuel long runs or race day** — this is non-negotiable. Deficit comes from rest-day and easy-day eating, not from skimping around hard workouts.
- **Protein first:** aim for roughly 0.7–1g of protein per lb of bodyweight daily to preserve muscle while losing fat.
- **Carbs scale with training load:** more carbs on long run/tempo days, fewer on rest days.
- **Hydrate consistently:** aim for pale-yellow urine; add electrolytes on long run days (especially in heat).
- **Don't fear food before/after key runs** — under-fueling here is the #1 cause of stalled weight loss + bad workouts (you compensate by overeating later or feeling too fatigued to train well).

### Daily Structure (example — adjust portions to your size/activity)

**Easy/Rest Days (lower carb need):**
| Meal | Example |
|---|---|
| Breakfast | Greek yogurt, berries, handful of nuts, drizzle of honey |
| Lunch | Large salad with grilled chicken or chickpeas, olive oil vinaigrette, whole-grain roll |
| Snack | Apple + 1 tbsp peanut butter |
| Dinner | Baked salmon or tofu, roasted vegetables, small portion quinoa or rice |
| Evening (if hungry) | Cottage cheese or a small handful of almonds |

**Hard Workout / Long Run Days (higher carb need):**
| Meal | Example |
|---|---|
| Pre-run (1–2 hrs before) | Oatmeal with banana and a little honey, or toast with jam |
| During long runs (>75-90 min) | Sports drink, gel, or chews every 30–45 min (~30-60g carbs/hr) |
| Post-run (within 30-60 min) | Recovery shake or chocolate milk + banana — protein + carbs |
| Lunch | Turkey or bean wrap, brown rice, vegetables |
| Dinner | Pasta or rice bowl with lean protein (chicken, fish, tofu) and lots of vegetables |
| Snacks | Fruit, yogurt, trail mix |

### Sample Weekly Calorie/Macro Targets *(adjust to your size — these are illustrative)*
- **Rest/Easy days:** ~80–90% of maintenance calories; protein 30%, carbs 35-40%, fat 25-30%
- **Long run/hard days:** at or slightly above maintenance; protein 20-25%, carbs 50-55%, fat 20-25%

### Foods to Emphasize
- Lean proteins: chicken, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, tofu, legumes
- Complex carbs: oats, brown rice, quinoa, sweet potatoes, whole grain bread/pasta
- Healthy fats: olive oil, avocado, nuts, seeds (in moderation — calorie dense)
- Plenty of vegetables and fruit for micronutrients and fiber/satiety
- Electrolyte-rich foods/drinks on long run days (bananas, electrolyte tabs, salty snacks)

### Foods to Limit (Not Eliminate)
- Sugary drinks and excess alcohol (empty calories, hurt recovery)
- Fried/highly processed foods on a regular basis
- Very large portions of refined carbs on non-training days

### Practical Tips
- Weigh yourself 1-2x/week, same time of day, and track the **trend** not daily fluctuation.
- If weight loss stalls for 2+ weeks despite consistent habits, reduce daily intake by another ~150-200 calories.
- If you're constantly fatigued, sleeping poorly, or workouts feel terrible — you're likely under-fueling. Eat more, especially carbs around training.
- Prioritize sleep (7-9 hrs) — it affects both recovery and appetite regulation as much as diet does.

## Race Week Checklist
- Carb-load lightly days 2-3 before race (no need for "carb loading" excess — just don't under-eat carbs)
- Hydrate well all week, especially with electrolytes
- Lay out gear, bib, fuel the night before
- Eat a familiar, carb-forward breakfast 2-3 hrs before race start
- Trust the training — you've earned this.
